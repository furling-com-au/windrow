## 1. Bunge — media / corporate affairs

*What in the draft concerns you:* the count of Bunge receival points and the roster behind
it; the 2025/26 operating status of individual sites; the drive-time finding; the 2019
Viterra closures and the reasons given for them; the corporate timeline; the Murdinga
tender; and a reported buyer list for T-Ports.

1. We count 22 grain receival points operated by Bunge on the Eyre Peninsula in 2025/26 —
   20 upcountry sites plus the terminals at Port Lincoln and Thevenard — built from Bunge's
   published segregations table and its weekly harvest reports. Is that count correct, and
   is there a site we have missed or included in error? We would take a corrected list in
   any form.
2. Our list does not include Tooligie, Cowell, Mangalo or Murdinga, although Viterra or
   Bunge weekly reports name the first three receiving grain in earlier seasons. Did any of
   them receive grain in 2025/26?
3. Kapinnie appears in Bunge's segregations table but we found no 2025/26 weekly-report
   mention of it. Did Kapinnie receive grain in 2025/26? The same question for Nunjikompita.
4. On 7 July 2026 Bunge offered six surplus South Australian bulk-handling sites for tender
   through Elders, including Murdinga (23,400 t) on the Eyre Peninsula, with binding offers
   due 21 August 2026. Has that tender concluded, and has Murdinga been sold? If so, does
   the buyer intend to operate it as a receival site?
5. Bunge's statement described those sites as no longer part of its operational network. Is
   that the correct wording, and would Bunge like to expand on it?
6. In June 2019 Viterra announced the closure of 17 up-country sites, six on the Eyre
   Peninsula. The reasons reported at the time were increased average truck size,
   quality-management and food-safety requirements, and those sites representing less than
   2 per cent of total receivals on a five-year average. Is that a complete and accurate
   statement of the reasons? Does Bunge wish to add anything about a decision taken before
   it acquired Viterra?
7. We date the completion of the Bunge/Viterra merger to 2 July 2025, and the renaming of
   Viterra Operations Pty Ltd to Bunge Operations Pty Ltd to 21 August 2025. Are both dates
   correct?
8. In August 2025 the Stock Journal reported that GrainCorp, CBH, Qube, Bunge and Louis
   Dreyfus were "tipped to top the potential buyers' list" for T-Ports. Has Bunge made, or
   is Bunge considering, any offer or approach for T-Ports or Australian Grain Export? A
   "no", or "we don't comment on market speculation", will be reported as given. *(The draft
   as it stands makes no assertion about Bunge's intentions; the earlier version did, and
   that sentence has been removed.)*
9. Our measure is drive time from production cells to the nearest receival point by owner.
   On our figures the tonne-weighted drive to the nearest Bunge point on the peninsula in
   2025/26 is a median of 16.5 minutes and a 99th percentile of 58.3. Does that match
   Bunge's own understanding of its catchment, and is there a published or shareable figure
   we should be using instead?
10. Is there anything in the draft that Bunge considers factually wrong? We will correct it.

## 2. T-Ports — CEO Nathan Kent

*What in the draft concerns you:* the 2025/26 closure of the Lock and Kimba bunkers and the
wording quoted; the 2023/24 operating status of both bunkers; site capacities; Lucky Bay's
description and location; the sale process; and how T-Ports' 2024 ACCC submission is
described.

1. Your harvest page currently shows "Site closed for 25/26 season" against both Lock and
   Kimba, with Lucky Bay and Wallaroo operating. Is that correct for both sites for 2025/26?
2. Did the Lock and Kimba bunkers receive grain in 2023/24? In 2024/25? We have found no
   published record either way, and our 2024/25 position is currently an assumption we would
   rather replace with a fact.
3. Will either bunker open for the 2026/27 season? Will Lucky Bay?
4. We use 384,000 t for Lucky Bay, 140,000 t for Lock and 150,000 t for Kimba, from your
   website and the ACCC's 2021 determinations. Are those the right figures?
5. Is there anything you would want said about *why* the bunkers are closed for 2025/26? The
   draft states expressly that the reason is not established, that this was T-Ports' own
   decision, and that a drought year and bunker economics are sufficient explanations. We
   would rather publish your account than leave the question open.
6. What share of Eyre Peninsula grain does T-Ports receive in a normal season? Nothing is
   published, and we have declined to publish our model's estimate. A figure, or a range,
   would replace an acknowledged gap.
7. Is the business still for sale as at today's date, and is the reported figure of about
   $250 million — or "offers of more than $200 million" — one that T-Ports or Nash Advisory
   recognises?
8. In October 2024 T-Ports lodged a submission to the ACCC opposing Viterra's proposed
   acquisition of the Grainflow sites. Would you provide a copy? We would rather describe
   what it argued than characterise it from press reports.
9. Our figures put the mean drive from an Eyre Peninsula production cell to Lucky Bay at
   about 125 minutes, and record its coordinate only to town precision — which is worth
   nearly six percentage points on the draft's central figure. If you can give us the
   facility coordinate, we will use it and republish the sensitivity.
10. Is anything in the draft wrong about T-Ports?

## 4. Grain Producers SA

*What concerns you:* how GPSA's position on the 2024 Grainflow proposal is described, and
the draft's characterisation of grower choice on the peninsula.

**Note on a correction already made.** An earlier version of this draft stated that GPSA
opposed Viterra's proposed acquisition of the Grainflow sites. That was wrong, and it has
been corrected before this was sent to anyone. The draft now says GPSA surveyed growers and
put their concerns to the ACCC without taking a position. We would like to check that this
is right.

1. Is that an accurate description of GPSA's position in October–December 2024?
2. Would GPSA provide, or point us to, its submission to the ACCC?
3. The draft's central measure is drive time from production cells to the nearest receival
   point not operated by Bunge. Is distance-to-an-alternative a measure GPSA regards as
   meaningful for its members, or is it the wrong question? We would rather be told it is
   the wrong question now than after publication.
4. The draft is careful to say it counts delivery points and not buyers, because growers
   also sell to traders, mills and packers through contracts executed at Bunge sites. In
   GPSA's view, how much does that distinction matter to a grower's real choice?
5. Does GPSA hold, or know of, any receival or throughput data for Eyre Peninsula sites at
   finer geography than the single regional total in the operator's weekly reports?
6. Is anything in the draft wrong, or missing, from the growers' side?
